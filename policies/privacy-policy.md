## 1. Purpose

This Privacy & Data Protection Policy establishes [COMPANY_NAME]'s commitment to protecting the privacy of individuals whose personal data it collects, processes, and stores. It defines the principles, obligations, and practices that ensure [COMPANY_NAME] handles personal data lawfully, transparently, and securely in accordance with applicable privacy regulations including PIPEDA, GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and other relevant data protection laws.

Privacy is not just a legal obligation. It is a trust obligation to customers, employees, and partners. This policy ensures that privacy considerations are embedded into how [COMPANY_NAME] operates, not treated as an afterthought.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All personal data collected, processed, stored, or transmitted by [COMPANY_NAME], regardless of format (electronic, paper, verbal)
- All employees, contractors, consultants, temporary workers, and interns who handle personal data on behalf of [COMPANY_NAME]
- All systems, applications, and third-party services that process personal data
- Personal data of customers, employees, prospective employees, partners, website visitors, and any other individuals
- Data processing activities conducted in all jurisdictions where [COMPANY_NAME] operates or serves customers

## 3. Policy Statements

### 3.1 Lawful Basis for Processing

[COMPANY_NAME] shall only process personal data when there is a valid lawful basis. The applicable lawful basis must be identified and documented before processing begins. Lawful bases include:

- **Consent:** The individual has given clear, informed, and freely given consent for a specific processing purpose. Consent must be as easy to withdraw as it was to give.
- **Contractual Necessity:** Processing is necessary to fulfill a contract with the individual or to take steps at their request before entering into a contract (e.g., providing the service they signed up for).
- **Legitimate Interest:** Processing is necessary for [COMPANY_NAME]'s legitimate business interests, provided those interests are not overridden by the individual's rights and freedoms. A legitimate interest assessment must be documented before relying on this basis.
- **Legal Obligation:** Processing is necessary to comply with a legal obligation to which [COMPANY_NAME] is subject (e.g., tax reporting, employment law requirements).
- **Vital Interests:** Processing is necessary to protect someone's life. This basis is rarely applicable in [COMPANY_NAME]'s context.
- **Public Interest:** Processing is necessary for a task carried out in the public interest. This basis is rarely applicable in [COMPANY_NAME]'s context.

The lawful basis for each category of processing activity shall be recorded in the Record of Processing Activities (ROPA), maintained by the Privacy Lead.

### 3.2 Data Subject Rights

[COMPANY_NAME] shall enable individuals to exercise their rights under applicable data protection laws. The following rights shall be supported:

- **Right of Access:** Individuals may request confirmation of whether their personal data is being processed and, if so, access to that data along with information about the processing purposes, categories of data, recipients, and retention periods.
- **Right to Rectification:** Individuals may request correction of inaccurate personal data or completion of incomplete data.
- **Right to Erasure (Right to be Forgotten):** Individuals may request deletion of their personal data where there is no compelling reason for continued processing, subject to legal retention requirements.
- **Right to Data Portability:** Where processing is based on consent or contract and carried out by automated means, individuals may request their personal data in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format, and may request that it be transmitted to another controller.
- **Right to Restrict Processing:** Individuals may request restriction of processing in certain circumstances (e.g., while accuracy is being verified or while an objection is being assessed).
- **Right to Object:** Individuals may object to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing purposes.
- **Right Not to be Subject to Automated Decision-Making:** Individuals have the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produce legal or similarly significant effects.

**Procedures for handling data subject requests:**
- All data subject requests must be submitted to [LEGAL_CONTACT] or through the mechanism published in [COMPANY_NAME]'s privacy notice.
- Requests must be acknowledged within 5 business days.
- Requests must be fulfilled within 30 calendar days (or within the timeframe required by the applicable regulation).
- Identity verification must be performed before disclosing personal data or making changes.
- Requests and their outcomes must be logged for compliance and audit purposes.
- If a request is denied (e.g., legal retention obligation), the individual must be informed of the reason and their right to complain to a supervisory authority.

### 3.3 Privacy Impact Assessments

A Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA), also referred to as a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA), must be conducted before:

- Launching a new product, feature, or service that involves processing personal data in a new way
- Implementing new technology that processes personal data (e.g., AI/ML systems, biometric systems, tracking technologies)
- Making significant changes to existing data processing activities
- Processing personal data on a large scale
- Processing special categories of personal data (health, biometric, racial/ethnic origin, political opinions, etc.)
- Engaging in systematic monitoring of individuals
- Cross-border transfers of personal data to new jurisdictions

**PIA requirements:**
- The PIA must describe the processing activity, its purpose, and the lawful basis
- It must assess the necessity and proportionality of the processing
- It must identify and assess risks to data subjects' rights and freedoms
- It must define measures to mitigate identified risks
- PIAs must be reviewed and approved by the Privacy Lead before the processing activity begins
- Completed PIAs shall be retained for audit purposes and reviewed when the processing activity changes

### 3.4 Cross-Border Data Transfers

When personal data is transferred outside the jurisdiction in which it was collected, [COMPANY_NAME] must ensure that adequate protections are in place:

- **Adequacy decisions:** Data may be transferred to jurisdictions that have been recognized as providing an adequate level of data protection by the relevant authority (e.g., EU adequacy decisions).
- **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs):** Where no adequacy decision exists, transfers must be governed by approved standard contractual clauses or equivalent transfer mechanisms.
- **Binding Corporate Rules:** For intra-group transfers, binding corporate rules may be used where approved by the relevant supervisory authority.
- **Transfer Impact Assessments:** A transfer impact assessment must be conducted to evaluate the legal framework of the receiving jurisdiction and whether supplementary measures are needed to ensure adequate protection.
- **Sub-processor obligations:** Third parties receiving personal data through cross-border transfers must be contractually bound to provide equivalent data protection.
- A register of cross-border data transfers shall be maintained by the Privacy Lead, identifying the data categories, source and destination jurisdictions, transfer mechanism, and safeguards in place.

### 3.5 Data Breach Notification

In the event of a personal data breach, [COMPANY_NAME] shall comply with all applicable notification obligations:

- **PIPEDA (Canada):** Breaches involving personal information that pose a real risk of significant harm must be reported to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and affected individuals as soon as feasible. Records of all breaches must be maintained for 24 months.
- **GDPR (EU/EEA):** Breaches likely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms must be reported to the relevant supervisory authority within **72 hours** of becoming aware of the breach. Affected individuals must be notified without undue delay where the breach is likely to result in high risk.
- **CCPA/CPRA (California):** Affected California residents must be notified in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay.
- **Other jurisdictions:** Notification shall comply with the requirements of all jurisdictions in which affected individuals reside.

**Breach notification procedures:**
- The Incident Response Policy governs the initial detection, containment, and investigation of data breaches.
- The Privacy Lead must be engaged immediately when a security incident involves or may involve personal data.
- The Privacy Lead, in coordination with Legal, shall determine notification obligations based on the nature and scope of the breach.
- Notifications to supervisory authorities shall include: the nature of the breach, categories and approximate number of individuals affected, likely consequences, and measures taken or proposed to address the breach.
- Notifications to affected individuals shall include: a description of the breach in plain language, contact information for questions, likely consequences, and measures taken to address the breach and mitigate harm.
- All breach notifications and related decisions must be documented for audit and regulatory purposes.

### 3.6 Privacy by Design and by Default

[COMPANY_NAME] shall embed privacy considerations into the design and operation of systems, products, and business practices:

- **Proactive not reactive:** Anticipate and prevent privacy-invasive events before they happen, rather than waiting for privacy risks to materialize.
- **Privacy as the default:** Personal data must be automatically protected in any system or business practice. Individuals should not have to take action to protect their privacy; it should be built in by default.
- **Data minimization:** Collect only the personal data that is necessary for the specified purpose. Do not collect data "just in case" it might be useful.
- **Purpose limitation:** Personal data collected for one purpose must not be repurposed without a valid lawful basis and, where required, additional consent.
- **Retention limitation:** Personal data must not be retained longer than necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, unless required by law.
- **Security:** Appropriate technical and organizational measures must be applied to protect personal data throughout its lifecycle.
- **Transparency:** Individuals must be informed about how their personal data is collected, used, and shared, in clear and accessible language.
- **User-centric design:** Privacy controls and options must be accessible and understandable to users.

Privacy by design requirements must be incorporated into the software development lifecycle, product design processes, and vendor selection criteria.

### 3.7 Record of Processing Activities

[COMPANY_NAME] shall maintain a Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) that documents:

- The name and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, the data protection officer
- The purposes of processing
- Categories of data subjects and categories of personal data
- Categories of recipients to whom personal data is disclosed
- Cross-border transfers and associated safeguards
- Retention periods for each category of data
- A general description of technical and organizational security measures

The ROPA shall be maintained by the Privacy Lead, reviewed quarterly, and made available to supervisory authorities upon request.

### 3.8 Privacy Notices

- [COMPANY_NAME] shall maintain privacy notices that inform individuals about how their personal data is collected, used, shared, retained, and protected.
- Privacy notices must be written in clear, plain language and be easily accessible.
- Separate privacy notices shall be maintained as needed for customers, employees, job applicants, and website visitors.
- Privacy notices must be reviewed and updated whenever processing activities change materially.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Privacy Lead** | Maintain this policy and the ROPA; conduct and oversee PIAs; manage data subject requests; advise on privacy compliance; coordinate breach notifications; monitor regulatory changes; serve as the point of contact for supervisory authorities. |
| **Security Lead** | Implement technical controls to protect personal data; support breach investigation and containment; maintain encryption, access controls, and monitoring relevant to personal data. |
| **Legal** | Advise on regulatory obligations across jurisdictions; review DPAs and SCCs; support breach notification decisions; ensure contractual privacy protections with vendors and partners. |
| **All Employees** | Handle personal data in accordance with this policy and applicable privacy notices; complete privacy training; report suspected privacy incidents to [SECURITY_TEAM_EMAIL]; apply data minimization in daily work. |
| **Engineering / Product** | Implement privacy by design in products and features; conduct PIAs for new processing activities; build data subject rights mechanisms into systems; ensure data minimization in system design. |
| **HR** | Ensure employee privacy data is handled in compliance with employment privacy laws; manage employee data subject requests; include privacy obligations in employment agreements; coordinate privacy training. |
| **Vendors / Third Parties** | Process personal data only as instructed by [COMPANY_NAME] and in accordance with DPAs; notify [COMPANY_NAME] of data breaches; comply with [COMPANY_NAME]'s privacy requirements. |

## 5. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must be requested in writing to the Privacy Lead with:

- The specific requirement being excepted
- Business justification
- Duration of the exception
- Risk assessment, including impact on data subjects
- Compensating controls in place
- Legal review confirming the exception does not violate applicable regulations

Exceptions must be approved by the Privacy Lead and Legal, and are tracked in the risk register.

## 6. Review Cadence

This policy shall be reviewed **annually** or upon:

- Changes to applicable privacy regulations
- Expansion into new jurisdictions
- Significant changes to data processing activities
- Privacy-related incidents or regulatory findings
- Changes to [COMPANY_NAME]'s products or services that materially affect personal data processing
